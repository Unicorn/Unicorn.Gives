General Comments by Chair: Chair Godau advised that Lincoln Township is a
zoned township, of only six in Clare County. It is important for people who move
into the township to understand that Lincoln is zoned and how the boards are
selected or elected and the structure under which those boards operate.

He explained that the ZBA is not a political structure, but one of appointment. He
went on to explain about what a variance involves and how the zoning
administrator deals with the steps of zoning permits, building permits, and then the
process in which a permit is denied. He also explained the roles of the zoning
administrator and the building inspector. Once an appeal is made to the ZBA and
denied or approved, any aggrieved person can appeal to the Circuit Court.

Purpose: Chair Godau read the following information: This is a variance request
for property #18-010-008-034-23, located at the S.W. Corner of Arthur Road and
Lake George Avenue, (a proposed site for Dollar General), requesting to provide
thirty-three (33) parking spaces versus the required number of forty-eight (48) per
Section 1513 – Parking, Table B – Minimum Off-street Parking Requirements,
Subpart 3N – Retail Stores. The notice of this variance was published in the Clare
County Cleaver on November 6, 2014, in accordance with the township zoning
ordinances. Notifications were sent to all property owners within 300 feet of the
property for the purpose of public comment being made to the ZBA. The public
hearing information was also posted at the township hall and on the township’s
website in the calendar of events.

Brief Explanation of the Rules: Chair Godau explained that variance requests are
submitted by the persons making the request. The Lincoln Township Board of
Appeals must follow the variance standards created by Michigan Statute. These
provide that the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in order to
grant a variance. These statutes ARE LAW and provide that: 1. There is a
Practical Difficulty; 2. The circumstances are Unique to the Property; 3. There is
Substantial Justice; 4. The issue was Not Self-Created; Minimum Variance
Necessary

Petitioner’s Statement: Peter Oleszczuk, developer with Midwest V, LLC,
explained that the company he will be leasing to, Dollar General has stated that
they only need 30 spaces, but that they added 3 more for ease of use. Mr.
Oleszczuk said that he and his partners desire to have minimal impact on the
existing site. They will leave the existing trees in place. The proposed parking
will provide adequate spaces for customers, employees, and enough space for the
maneuvering of semis that deliver goods. He summed up the request that they are
asking for only the 33 spaces for not only economic issues, but also to lessen the
impact on the environment.

Finding of Fact: The board finalized their finding of facts and turned them in to

Final Decision: Godau requested a motion from the board. Bridges made the
motion to allow relief from the existing zoning ordinance requirements and allow
Dollar General to create 33 parking spaces instead of the 48 required under the
formula in the Lincoln Township Zoning Ordinance. This relief shall apply only to
the Dollar General business (not the building) and only as long as Dollar General
occupies this building. If Dollar General fails to occupy this building, the parking
requirements shall revert back to the required parking spaces calculated by the
current Lincoln Township Zoning Ordinance formula. Szczepanski seconded.

Godau explained that this motion requires a yea or nay in support of the motion.


Roll Call Vote:
Godau: Yay; Swanson: Yay; Bridges: Yay; Engelhardt: Yay; Szczepanski: Yay
Motion Passed: 5 Yays; 0 nays. Variance is granted.
