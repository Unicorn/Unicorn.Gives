The Lincoln Township Board of Appeals must follow the variance standards
created by Michigan Statute and Ordinance 44 of Lincoln Twp. In accordance
with these standards the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in
order to grant a variance. Practical Difficulties as identified in Section8.7 of the
Lincoln Township Zoning Ordinances ARE THE LAW. They are as follows:

                  1. Practical Difficulty: That there are practical difficulties that
                     prevent carrying out the strict letter of this Ordinance due to
                     unique circumstances specific to the property such as its
                     narrowness, shallowness, shape, or topography, and which do
                     not generally apply to other property or uses in the same
                     district and shall not be recurrent in nature. That a genuine
                     hardship exists due to the circumstances are Unique to the
                     Property
                  2. Circumstances: That the practical difficulty or special
                     condition or circumstance is not a result of the actions of the
                     applicant. That the variance will relate only to property
                     described in the variance application. That granting the
                     variance will not grant an applicant any special privilege.
                  3. Ordinance Purpose Adherence: Does this request relate to
                     properties other than described in the Variance request?
                  4. Variance Harmony: That the variance will be in harmony with
                     the purpose of this Ordinance and the intent of the District,
                     including the protection of public health, safety and welfare in
                     general and vehicular and pedestrian circulation specifically.
                  5. Effects: That the variance will not cause a substantial adverse
                     effect upon surrounding property including property values
                     and the development, use and enjoyment of property in the
                     neighborhood or District.
                  6. Property Use: That strict compliance with the site
                     development requirement in question would unreasonably
                     prevent the owner from using the property for a permitted
                     purpose or would render conformity unnecessarily
                     burdensome.
                  7. Minimum Variance: That the variance requested is the
                     minimum amount necessary to overcome the inequality
                     inherent in the particular property or mitigate the practical
                     difficulty.

less than five (5) feet.

5. Front Yard Setback Measurements and Special Water Setbacks:
a.     Measurement: Unless specified otherwise in this Ordinance, including (b)
below, front yard setbacks shall be measured from the road right-of-way line
constituting the front lot line.
b.     Waterfront Lot: In the case of a waterfront lot, the front yard shall be that
yard adjacent to the water body and the front yard setback shall be measured from
the ordinary high water mark or, where a shoreline wall is present, from the face of
the shoreline wall. See Article 21 for definitions of “yard, front” and “ordinary
high water mark”.
c.     Ordinary High Water Mark Setback: In no case shall a structure be located

closer to the lake than the ordinances allow. They are not asking for a large deck,
just one large enough to sit and have coffee while watching activity on the lake.
Their lot’s angle makes the deck come too close to the lake.

and accepts a motion to deny or approve the Variance Request the applicant may
wait to receive a final decision paper for court purposes. If the applicant does not
want to wait for the final decision paper, the decision will not be final until after
the next ZBA meeting. In the case of a hearing held via Zoom, the Certified
Decision Form will be emailed to the applicant in order to expedite the process.
