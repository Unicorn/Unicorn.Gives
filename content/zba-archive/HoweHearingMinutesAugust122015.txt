He explained that the ZBA is not a political structure, but one of appointment. He
went on to explain about what a variance involves and how the zoning

Purpose: Chair Godau read the following information: This is a variance request
for James Howe pertaining to his property at 1412 Arbor Drive, Lake George,
Michigan, for the placement of an Accessory Building which violates the required
minimum five (5) foot setback from the property line to structure (Accessory
Building).

Public notice: Notice of this Public Hearing was posted in the Clare County
Cleaver (Legal Section) on July 23, 2015. Notifications were sent to all property
owners within 300 feet of the property for the purpose of public comment being
made to the ZBA. The public hearing information was also posted at the township
hall and on the township’s website in the calendar of events.

Brief Explanation of the Rules: Chair Godau explained that variance requests are
submitted by the persons making the request. The Lincoln Township Board of
Appeals must follow the variance standards created by Michigan Statute. These
provide that the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in order to
grant a variance. These statutes ARE LAW and provide that: 1. There is a
Practical Difficulty; 2. The circumstances are Unique to the Property; 3. There is
Substantial Justice; 4. The issue was Not Self-Created; 5. Minimum Variance
Necessary

Petitioner’s Statement: Mr. James Howe state that almost 3 years ago there was a
slab on his property that was there when he purchased it and it looked bad, even if
he tried to cover it with tarps and other materials to hide it. He saw some sheds by
K&K and called to ask if he needed a permit to put up one of these sheds. Ken
Osborne, the contractor, said he didn’t need a permit. He protests that to place the
building in any other place, he will have to cut down more trees. He explained that
to move the shed in any way, it will cause a hardship in maneuvering his boat into
the shed. Also when he drops off wood during different times in the year, moving
the shed will make it much more difficult to unload his wood. He contends that it
looks much better than what was there previously including the tarps that he used
before the shed. He presented letters from neighbors on either side of his property
which are in support of his shed and have no objections to the shed’s placement.
He stated that if Ken Osborne had indicated that he would need a permit and that
there might be some problems in his desired placement, he would have been able
to do something different. He claims that there is no other option with the lot’s
limitations.

He feels that a 40 foot lot presents a practical difficulty that is unique. Unique to
the property is the pre-existing slab in that spot. Another practical difficulty is the
number of trees that he would have to remove. He has already removed 11 trees.
He feels that he has been supporting the natural resources in support of the mission
of the ordinances. He then cited the neighbors and how they all cooperate and get
along with taking care of their lands.

He feels he is stuck as the shape of the shed is such that it couldn’t be reduced on
either side. It is extremely heavy so that it will be next to impossible to move. He
stated that Mr. Carey follows the rules of a 5 foot setback and has no wiggle room
here, so that is why he is appealing to the board.

He said that it has been this way for 3 years and doesn’t know who complained or
why. He has invested $4,000 on this and if you tear it down, he is out that much.
He said that back several years when he built his house, he was told no zoning was
required at that time. For all of that he followed all the rules for that house and his
other structures and assumed that the same would apply to this shed.

Attachment #1


                             (Zoning Administrator's Report)
On 04/17/15 I received a complaint that the owner of the property at 1412 Arbor
Dr. had added a shed to his property with no Zoning Permit or Building Permit in
violation of our Zoning Ordinance. I notified Mr. Howe, of 1412 Arbor Dr. that he
needed to apply for a Zoning Permit and that I would turn it down as it was in
violation of our setbacks and Mr. Howe would then have to move the shed to
come into compliance with our Zoning Ordinance or apply for a Variance
The ZBA should grant a variance from the township's zoning laws only after the
property in question meets stringent tests described in the zoning ordinance.
Only the rarest situations should qualify for relief from the zoning ordinance's
requirements because the zoning regulations are ultimately intended to protect
the general health, safety and welfare of the township.


Practical Difficulty: Lincoln Township has a set of criteria an applicant must meet
to be allowed a Variance. An applicant must meet each of the 5 criteria's for a
variance, not 4 or 3, all 5 practical difficulties. Michigan appellate court has held
that the Zoning Board of Appeals cannot grant a Variance without substantial
evidence of all 5 practical difficulties.
For number one, Practical Difficulty, Mr. Howe says lot is very narrow and
access to back lot would be cut off. Mr. Howe’s shed is 24 X 24 and his lot is
40 feet wide. If Mr. Howe moves the shed to comply with our Ordinance, he

on the other side more than enough room. There is no Practical Difficulty.
For number two Mr. Howe, states the placement of trees prevent moving the

Number four, not self-created, Mr. Howe contends his contractor told him a
Permit was not needed. Mr. Howe should have consulted the Zoning
Administrator. Our Zoning Ordinance says an accessory building or structure,
(both of which require a Zoning Permit), on the same lot or contiguous lot as
the main residence must follow setbacks. It further defines a building as any
structure having a roof supported by columns or walls for the shelter of
persons, animals or property of any kind. That said this issue was self-created
and is not a practical difficulty.
Number five, minimum variance necessary, there is no Practical Difficulty so
this also does not apply.
