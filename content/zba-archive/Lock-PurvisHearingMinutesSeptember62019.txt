Explanation of Practical Difficulty: Chair Carey read the following information:

The Lincoln Township Board of Appeals must follow the variance standards
created by Michigan Statute and Ordinance 44 of Lincoln Twp. In accordance
with these standards the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in
order to grant a variance. Practical Difficulties as identified in Section8.7 of the
Lincoln Township Zoning Ordinances ARE THE LAW. They are as follows:
                  1. Practical Difficulty: That there are practical difficulties that
                      prevent carrying out the strict letter of this Ordinance due to
Zoning Board of Appeals Minutes           3                                 9/6/2019


                     unique circumstances specific to the property such as its
                     narrowness, shallowness, shape, or topography, and which do
                     not generally apply to other property or uses in the same
                     district and shall not be recurrent in nature. That a genuine
                     hardship exists due to the circumstances are Unique to the
                     Property
                  2. Circumstances: That the practical difficulty or special
                     condition or circumstance is not a result of the actions of the
                     applicant. That the variance will relate only to property
                     described in the variance application. That granting the
                     variance will not grant an applicant any special privilege.
                  3. Ordinance Purpose Adherence: Does this request relate to
                     properties other than described in the Variance request?
                  4. Variance Harmony: That the variance will be in harmony with
                     the purpose of this Ordinance and the intent of the District,
                     including the protection of public health, safety and welfare in
                     general and vehicular and pedestrian circulation specifically.
                  5. Effects: That the variance will not cause a substantial adverse
                     effect upon surrounding property including property values
                     and the development, use and enjoyment of property in the
                     neighborhood or District.
                  6. Property Use: That strict compliance with the site
                     development requirement in question would unreasonably
                     prevent the owner from using the property for a permitted
                     purpose or would render conformity unnecessarily
                     burdensome.
                  7. Minimum Variance: That the variance requested is the
                     minimum amount necessary to overcome the inequality
                     inherent in the particular property or mitigate the practical
                     difficulty.

Correspondence & Communications to ZBA: Chair Carey read five letters or

Presentation by Lincoln Township Zoning Administrator: Acting ZA
Majewski reviewed the letter which is included above in the section “Denial of
Zoning Application”. She spoke briefly about her site visit findings.

Presentation by the Petitioner: Ms. Tammie Locke-Purvis explained that the
projects are both unfinished and they have stopped all work after former ZA
Abbott served a cease and desist order. The only reason they put the shed where
they did was to avoid a loss of view of the lake by putting it in their front yard and
also because there was already a falling down metal shed where they began
building their new one. Skip Purvis explained that the fence was put up quickly to
contain their dogs.

ZBA Members Comments & Questions: Engelhardt noted that they purchased
the property in March of 2019. They stated that they were not familiar with it
before then and had not owned lake property prior to this. Engelhardt went on to
explain the reason for setbacks, including fire hazards. She explained how
important ordinances are for lake properties as they are often so close together and
emphasized that she is glad that they stopped construction when they did.

Swanson asked if they will turn the fence around so the finished side faces the
neighbors and make it lower. Tammie replied that, yes, they intend to turn it
around and shorten it if they have to.

Chair Carey asked if anyone explained to them that they can have a taller 48”
cyclone (chain link) fence. They were not aware of that fact but did not consider
that to be as nice looking as their current choice.

Motions: Carey made the following motion:

In regard to Variance requested by Tammie and Jana Locke-Purvis on lot 010-640-
002-00, Section 7 of West Old Grade plot, lot 2, owned by Tammie and Jana
Locke-Purvis, address, 466 Shingle Lake Rd, Lake, MI 48632. The variance
request is for a shed in the backyard in violation of Lincoln Township Ordinance
44, Section 20.9 Accessory Buildings, Paragraph C-2 which says due to the size
this accessory building must comply with Table 3-4, backyard setback 25 feet from
back lot line and side setback of 10 feet from the side lot line. Also, Paragraph C-
3 which says Separation from another building, an accessory building shall not be
located within 10 feet of another building. The Variance also requests a picket
fence 42 inches in height in violation of Section 20.11, Fences and Walls,
Paragraph a, Residential Fences number2 which says no fence in the front yard
measured from ground up shall exceed 3 feet in height.

Review standards

Practical Difficulty: as the property can sustain a shed in the front yard and a 48-
inch-high cyclone fence there is no practical difficulty

Circumstances: there are no special circumstances due to the actions of the
applicant, the lot can be used as is and follow the ordinance.
Zoning Board of Appeals Minutes           7                                 9/6/2019


Ordinance Purpose Adherence: based on the review standards demonstrated by the
applicant, the variance would not observe the spirit of the zoning ordinance,
providing for the orderly development of the township, including making
provisions for land uses in the best interests of its citizens

Variance Harmony: in granting the Variance the purpose (harmony) of the Lincoln
Township Zoning Ordinance would not be observed.

Effects: in allowing the variance it would have an adverse effect on the neighbor
who aren’t allowed to have a shed in their backyard closer than 25 feet, or a picket
fence higher than 36 inches in their front yard.

Property use: no, the property owner can place the shed in the front yard and put up
the proper size fence.

Minimum Variance: there is no need for a variance as the applicant can legally do
what they want without a variance.

That said we find the applicants’ review standards do not justify a variance.

I make a Motion based on the review standards that the Zoning Board of Appeals
Deny the applicants’ Variance Request for a shed in the back yard and a 42 inch
high picket fence in the front yard of 466 Shingle Lake Rd.

Engelhardt seconded.
