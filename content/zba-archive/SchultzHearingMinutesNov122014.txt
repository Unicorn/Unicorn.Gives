General Comments by Chair: Chair Godau advised that Lincoln Township is a
zoned township, of only six in Clare County. It is important for people who move
into the township to understand that Lincoln is zoned and how the boards are
selected or elected and the structure under which those boards operate.
He explained that the ZBA is not a political structure, but one of appointment. He
went on to explain about what a variance involves and how the zoning
administrator deals with the steps of zoning permits, building permits, and then the

Purpose: Chair Godau read the following information: To consider the
September 21, 2014, application of Alan and Jane Schultz for a Request for
Variance for property #18-010-460-014-01 located at 210 Bringold Ave., Lake
George, Michigan, for the construction of a covered deck and walkway which
violates the fifty foot (50’) setback from the water’s edge (Shingle Lake). The
notice of this variance was published in the Clare County Cleaver on October 30,
2014, in accordance with the township zoning ordinances. Notifications were sent
to all property owners within 300 feet of the property for the purpose of public
comment being made to the ZBA. The public hearing information was also posted
at the township hall and on the township’s website in the calendar of events.

Brief Explanation of the Rules: Chair Godau explained that variance requests are
submitted by the persons making the request. The Lincoln Township Board of
Appeals must follow the variance standards created by Michigan Statute. These
provide that the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in order to
grant a variance. These statutes ARE LAW and provide that: 1. There is a
Practical Difficulty; 2. The circumstances are Unique to the Property; 3. There is
Substantial Justice; 4. The issue was Not Self-Created; Minimum Variance
Necessary

Practical Difficulty: Lincoln Township has a set of criteria an applicant must meet
to be allowed a Variance. An applicant must meet each of the 5 criteria’s for a
variance, not 4 or 3, all 5 practical difficulties. Michigan appellate court has held
that the Zoning Board of Appeals cannot grant a Variance without substantial
evidence of all 5 practical difficulties. The Michigan Court of Appeals has also
adopted the following factor, whether compliance with the strict letter of the
restrictions governing area, setbacks, frontage, height, bulk or density would
unreasonably prevent the owner from using the property for a permitted purpose.
This property can be used for its permitted purpose.

For number one Mr. Schultz says, being located at the end of the lake the house is
set back as far as it can go to meet and exceed the 50 foot setback. Mr. Schultz
knew there was not enough room for a deck before he even put the home in. The
following came from a zoning article I recently read: quote “the proper
interpretation is to ask whether the applicant took some affirmative action that
created the need for the variance. Filling the entire building envelope so that a
porch must necessarily extend into the setback area is not a Practical Difficulty”
unquote. There are other routes Mr. Schultz could take.

For number two Mr. Schultz says, the end of the lake, in a cove, the waterfront is
uniquely shaped problematic to work with. Mr. Schultz, aside from his deck had
more than enough room for his home, there is no practical difficulty.

Number three, Mr. Schultz says, because his deck does not obstruct anyone’s view
of the lake. Right of view is not a legal issue in Michigan this is not a practical
difficulty.

Number four Mr. Schultz says, the uniqueness of the lake restricts and being on the
corner he had to adhere to the road right of way. This was not a practical difficulty
as Mr. Schultz bought the property this way, nothing changed on the property, this
issue was self-created and is not a practical difficulty.

Number five, minimum variance necessary, aside from the front deck, which there
are other ways to handle, Mr. Schultz has been allowed all he has asked for. As for

a practical difficulty.

The bottom line is Mr. Schultz knew there was no room for a structure before he
even put the deck in and now afterwards he is claiming practical difficulty.
