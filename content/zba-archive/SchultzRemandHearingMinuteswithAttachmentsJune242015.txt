General Comments by Chair: Chair Godau summarized the purpose of this
meeting because the prior meeting was held in November 2014.

Lincoln Township is a Zoned Township. Of sixteen townships in Clare, ten are
zoned, six are not.

Lincoln Township is fortunate to have a separate Zoning Administrator and
Building Inspector.

It is well documented that a building project must have a Zoning permit (approved)
before a building permit can be issued.

Therefore, the first person on the scene is the Zoning Administrator. This did
occur in June 2014 (two occurrences) with the Owner and Builder. Actual
measurements were taken to assure proper location of structure and adherence to
the fifty (50) foot setback from water’s edge.

Site plan submitted by Owner/Builder was for the manufactured home. It did not
show any other decks, walkways, or construction.

Builder originally applied for a building permit through Clare County on June 11,
2014. The Clare County Building Department does not have jurisdiction in
Lincoln Township. The builder applying for the building permit was directed to
Lincoln Township.

Approximately three plus weeks later the Builder or the Owner contacted the
Lincoln Township Building Inspector.

On July 1, 2014, a phone discussion with the Builder’s Representative and
Building Inspector took place.

In efforts to make up lost time the Building Inspector issued a permit with
assurance from the Builder’s Representative that they had a valid zoning permit.
They did not.

Since the manufactured home has three (3) openings, and two (2) decks were asked
for, the assumption was that the larger deck would go on the north door and the
smaller on the east door.

No five foot (5’) walkway was requested at that time.

      a. Zoning Administrator’s Report: See Attachment #1
             ZA Carey read the definitions of deck and porch and proceeded to
describe the existing structures in question.

       b. Presentation on behalf of applicants
       Attorney Todd Grant presented photos of the structures in question. He
stated that ZA Carey is correct in that the existing definitions in the ordinances

must be followed. He explained that rule #1 is to look at the legal definitions and
not go by what the structures are commonly being called. In rule #2 he referred to
the way courts interpret rules and statutes. An open porch, by definition, is a
covered entrance; the structure projects out from the main structure. It has an
attached or integral roof. Definition of porch doesn’t say that after a certain size it
becomes a party deck. Definition of deck says that it can be attached or not as long
as it is raised up and supported; it is a deck. There are no limitations of size for a
deck. He stated that there is a difference in the zoning of this property being R1 or
R2.

Carey stated that Mr. Schultz entered R1 on the zoning permit. The Zoning
Administrator and the Township ZBA did consider this parcel as R2 in their
decision making process.

Godau disagreed in that the builder applied for a deck; called it a deck. This is the
generic sense of a deck. It is not simply an entrance into or out of the house.

Grant: you said that the builder applied for a deck. Just because people call it a
deck, doesn’t make it a deck. You have to look at the legal definition. Perhaps the
builder didn’t read the zoning ordinance and therefore called it a deck mistakenly.

Godau then read the zoning ordinance’s description of a deck. (See Zoning
Administrator’s Attachment #1)

Grant referred back to the concept that the specific will lead the general. Therefore
the definition of porch as having a roof makes it more specific.

Godau: if you use the porch definition then you could conceivably build it all the
way to the lake.

Grant: no, because you would still have to abide by the setbacks.

Grant: the second point is that it has been stated that because of the size it is solely
for the recreation. But recreation is not stated in the ordinance. The assumption on
your part is that due to the size it will be used for recreation.
A porch goes beyond a simple covered area to enter the house; it is for sitting on
for leisure.

Another point is that the porch is a subset of a deck. Decks and porches are
different items and are treated that way in the ordinance.

Issue/Question #3 – Should the Township be estopped from denying the requested
variance because it granted a building permit on July 1, 2014, which referenced the
subject deck?
      a. Zoning Administrator’s Report: Attachment #3
             ZA Carey read his statement that the township should not be estopped
from denying the requested variance.
      b. Presentation on behalf of applicants
Attorney Grant cited a case in another county where an inadvertent granting of a
permit caused a township to be estopped and lost the case.

Grant: Building Inspector Mantei, around July 1, granted a permit which refers to
two covered decks. The township knew there were two decks in early July even
though they weren’t included on the site plan. You can’t just walk out of a house
without an entry way, so everyone knew there would be decks even though they
were not on the site plan. The builder was surprised by this as she assumed that
the Zoning Administrator and Building Inspector both knew about the decks being
included in the plans. Alan Schultz was not told until the end of August that there
was a building problem. This was after the construction was finished. Even
though there are bit and pieces of information missing in the paperwork, I am sure
that Building Inspector Mantei was aware of the decks and was making visits to
the site. If something had been said in July, something could have been adjusted.
This is something that should have been brought to their attention sooner.

Godau: The first step is a valid zoning permit. Zoning Administrator Carey spent
time with the owner and told him clearly that you cannot extend into the 50 foot
setback to the water’s edge. We knew he had to do something to satisfy his three
openings. He had a hidden agenda. He had an old deck that he was going to just
push up against the third door. So there was some misleading in his head as he had
to come up with something for the third opening. It all comes down to one basic
