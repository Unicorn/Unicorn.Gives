General Comments by Chair: Chair Godau advised that Lincoln Township is a
zoned township, of only six in Clare County. It is important for people who move
into the township to understand that Lincoln is zoned and how the boards are
selected or elected and the structure under which those boards operate. The
township board is elected and serves in the legislative capacity. The planning
commission is appointed by the board and serves as the executive body.
He explained that the ZBA is not a political structure, but one of appointment as
well, and serves as the judicial body. He went on to explain about what a variance
involves and how the zoning administrator deals with the steps of zoning permits,
Zoning Board of Appeals Minutes          2                                   3/11/15


building permits, and then the process in which a permit is denied. He also
explained the roles of the zoning administrator and the building inspector. Once an
appeal is made to the ZBA and denied or approved, any aggrieved person can
appeal to the Circuit Court.

Purpose: Chair Godau read the following information: This is a variance request
by Lorraine Perry for her property, #18-010-330-001-10, located at 1490 Bungo
Trail, Lake George, Michigan, for the construction of awnings/roofs covering
existing decks which violates the existing Township Zoning Ordinance pertaining
to adding structures to existing non-conforming structures. Lorraine Perry’s
structure is a non-conforming structure due to its failure to meet the minimum
required setbacks from both roads. The notice of this variance was published in the
Clare County Cleaver on February 19, 2015, in accordance with the township
zoning ordinances. Notifications were sent to all property owners within 300 feet
of the property for the purpose of public comment being made to the ZBA. The
public hearing information was also posted at the township hall and on the
township’s website in the calendar of events.

Brief Explanation of the Rules: Chair Godau explained that variance requests are
submitted by the persons making the request. The Lincoln Township Board of
Appeals must follow the variance standards created by Michigan Statute. These
provide that the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in order to
grant a variance. These statutes ARE LAW and provide that: 1. There is a
Practical Difficulty; 2. The circumstances are Unique to the Property; 3. There is
Substantial Justice; 4. The issue was Not Self-Created; 5. Minimum Variance
Necessary

Attachment #1 (Zoning Administrator’s Report)

On 11/18/14 I received a complaint that the owner of the property at 1490 Bungo
Tr. had added a roof to their deck in violation of our Zoning Ordinance as she had
not applied for a Zoning Permit. On 11/20/14 I notified Lorraine Perry that her
roofs over her decks were in violation of or Zoning Ordinance, no Zoning Permit
was issued. They were also in violation as our ordinance says: Section 1300
Purpose, Section 1512 Paragraph 1 Intent and Paragraph 4a Non-conforming
Structure all say pretty much the same, no structure shall be erected, nor shall an
existing building be altered or enlarged unless it conforms with the minimum
setback requirements established for each district of this ordinance.
The ZBA should grant a variance from the township's zoning laws only after the
property in question meets stringent tests described in the zoning ordinance. Only
the rarest situations should qualify for relief from the zoning ordinance's
requirements because the zoning regulations are ultimately intended to protect
the general health, safety and welfare of the township.
Practical Difficulty: Lincoln Township has a set of criteria an applicant must meet
to be allowed a Variance. An applicant must meet each of the 5 criteria's for a
variance, not 4 or 3, all 5 practical difficulties. Michigan appellate court has held
that the Zoning Board of Appeals cannot grant a Variance without substantial
evidence of all 5 practical difficulties. The Michigan Court of Appeals has also
adopted the following factor, whether compliance with the strict letter of the
restrictions governing area, setbacks, frontage, height, bulk or density would
unreasonably prevent the owner from using the property for a permitted purpose.
This property can be used for its permitted purpose.
For number one Mrs. Perry says she doesn’t feel this applies as she has not
increased her non-conformity. By adding the roofs/awnings she has increased her
non-conforming structure. She provides no Practical Difficulty.
For number two Mrs. Perry says this does not apply to her and she quotes Page
56, Section 1512, Paragraph 3a. This paragraph applies to, non-conforming uses
of land. Mrs. Perry violated Page 56, Section 1512, Paragraph 4a, which says:
No such nonconforming structure may be enlarged or altered in a way which
increases its nonconformity. There is no Unique Situation.
Number three, Mrs. Perry provides no Substantial Justice.

increase her non-conforming structure by adding roofs/awnings. There was no
practical difficulty as Mrs. Perry bought the property this way, nothing changed on
the property, this issue was self-created and is not a practical difficulty.
Number five, minimum variance necessary, there is no Practical Difficulty so this
