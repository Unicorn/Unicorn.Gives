Purpose: Chair Godau read the following information: This is a variance request
for Russell Veenstra on his property at 301 West Park St., Lincoln Township,
Lake, MI 48632, for the construction of a partial wall on an open porch which
violates the existing Township Zoning Ordinance pertaining to adding structure to
existing non-conforming structure. Russell Veenstra’s is a non-conforming
structure due to its failure to meet the minimum required setbacks from both roads.

Public notice: Notice of this Public Hearing was posted in the Clare County
Cleaver (Legal Section) on July 21, 2016. Notifications were sent to all property
owners within 300 feet of the property for the purpose of public comment being
made to the ZBA. The public hearing information was also posted at the township
hall and on the township’s website in the calendar of events.

Brief Explanation of the Rules: Chair Godau explained that variance requests are
submitted in person by the persons making the request. The Lincoln Township
Board of Appeals must follow the variance standards created by Michigan Statute.
These provide that the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in
order to grant a variance. These statutes ARE LAW and provide that: 1. There is a
Practical Difficulty; 2. The circumstances are Unique to the Property; 3. There is
Substantial Justice; 4. The issue was Not Self-Created; 5. Minimum Variance
Necessary

Petitioner’s Statement: Mr. Veenstra explained his problem with water coming
into his house from the road since he purchased the property 27 years ago. He
called the road commission who told him to talk to the township. He said they just
kept passing the buck back and forth. During the spring his house gets flooded.
He has tried several methods fixing the problem. For several years he put up tarps
to try to solve the problem but there were complaints from neighbors about the
looks. In 2014 he put up boards on the side as well as a rain gutter on the north
end of the house. These two solutions seemed to help with the water problem. In
2015 he decided to add the stone to continue solving the problem. He kept calling
the road commission and the township but neither would help. He has dealt with
mold and other issues with water. He has even tried to use flowers and plants to
help to divert the water away from the low area. He states that he did not create
the problem. He says he is not the type to keep bugging people about problems,
but goes ahead and tries to fix things himself. He did not think he was breaking
any rules, but had to do something to keep from having to replace rugs. It has
taken a while to get the mold and mildew out of his house. Since he put the wall
up, he has not had any more water come into his house even though some has
come up on to his porch.

Attachment #1

I’m going to start my report by stating that, I’m not going to defend my positions
to the complainant, however I will answer any question directed to me by the
Zoning Board of Appeals.

Our Zoning Ordinance is the law. Mr. Veenstra added/altered his structure by
adding a half wall. This violates our Zoning Ordinance. Two places in our
Ordinance it says “No structure shall be erected, nor shall an existing building be
altered or enlarged unless it conforms with the minimum area and setback
requirements”.

The ZBA should grant a variance from the township's zoning laws only after the
property in question meets stringent tests described in the zoning ordinance. The
zoning regulations are ultimately intended to protect the general health, safety and
welfare of the township. Practical Difficulty: Lincoln Township has a set of
criteria an applicant must meet to be allowed a Variance. An applicant must
meet each of the 5 criteria's for a variance, not 4 or 3, all 5 practical difficulties.

For Practical Difficulty: Mr. Veenstra says his house has been flooding for 27
years. Michigan has a law that protects residents against this type of action, it is
called the Drain Law of Michigan. Why hasn’t he taken this up with the
County? If he has where is the documentation?

Mr. Veenstra claims to have had water damage, where is the documentation?

How is a wood framed wall going to keep water out? How is a wood framed
wall with one side open going to keep water out?

Unique Situation: I see nothing unique about this situation.

Substantial Justice: Mr. Veenstra admits he simply attached a knee wall/half
wall to the front of his house which already had a roof and cement floor. He
states he did not close it in on the ends. This is adding/altering a nonconforming
structure which violates our Zoning Ordinance.
