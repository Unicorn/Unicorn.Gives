Explanation of Practical Difficulty: Acting Chair McCaslin read the following
statement:

The Lincoln Township Board of Appeals must follow the variance standards
created by Michigan Statute and Ordinance 44 of Lincoln Twp. In accordance
with these standards the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in
order to grant a variance. Practical Difficulties as identified in Section8.7 of the
Lincoln Township Zoning Ordinances ARE THE LAW. They are as follows:
Zoning Board of Appeals Minutes           3                                12/9/2020


                  1. Practical Difficulty: That there are practical difficulties that
                     prevent carrying out the strict letter of this Ordinance due to
                     unique circumstances specific to the property such as its
                     narrowness, shallowness, shape, or topography, and which do
                     not generally apply to other property or uses in the same
                     district and shall not be recurrent in nature. That a genuine
                     hardship exists due to the circumstances are Unique to the
                     Property
                  2. Circumstances: That the practical difficulty or special
                     condition or circumstance is not a result of the actions of the
                     applicant. That the variance will relate only to property
                     described in the variance application. That granting the
                     variance will not grant an applicant any special privilege.
                  3. Ordinance Purpose Adherence: Does this request relate to
                     properties other than described in the Variance request?
                  4. Variance Harmony: That the variance will be in harmony with
                     the purpose of this Ordinance and the intent of the District,
                     including the protection of public health, safety and welfare in
                     general and vehicular and pedestrian circulation specifically.
                  5. Effects: That the variance will not cause a substantial adverse
                     effect upon surrounding property including property values
                     and the development, use and enjoyment of property in the
                     neighborhood or District.
                  6. Property Use: That strict compliance with the site
                     development requirement in question would unreasonably
                     prevent the owner from using the property for a permitted
                     purpose or would render conformity unnecessarily
                     burdensome.
                  7. Minimum Variance: That the variance requested is the
                     minimum amount necessary to overcome the inequality
                     inherent in the particular property or mitigate the practical
                     difficulty.

Presentation by Lincoln Township Zoning Administrator:

I have denied the application for a zoning permit for an accessory building at 1950
Forest Blvd. lot # 010-300-009-00 for the following reason: Failure to meet Road
Setbacks. Setbacks are 25 feet from the road on lake front property.
Our Zoning Ordinance clearly states, according to Table 3-4: accessory buildings
and structures greater than one-hundred twenty (120) square feet in area and/or
greater than ten (10) feet in height shall comply with the minimum side and rear
yard setback standards for the principal building according to the District in which
it is located, but in no case shall such side yard setback be less than five (5) feet.
According to Table 3-4, Site Development Requirements, setbacks for your size
shed are 25 feet from the Road Right of Way. The Road Right of Way is 40 feet on
this property (20 feet from the center of the road.)

A viable option for this property would be to construct the Accessory Building in
the Side Yard.

According to Table 3-4, Site Development Requirements, R-2 and R-2A Districts:
Side yard setbacks for principal buildings in the R-2 and R-2A Districts shall be
10’ except that in the case of a lot recorded with the County Register of Deeds
prior to the effective date of this Ordinance, that does not have a minimum lot
width of one-hundred (100) feet at some point measured between the normally
required front and rear yard setback distances according to Table 3-4, each
required minimum side yard setback shall be equal to ten percent (10%) of the lot
width but in no case shall a side yard setback be less than five (5) feet.
This means your rear yard setback would be:
•      25 feet from the road right of way
•      6.6 feet from side lot line calculated as such.
*      The property is 66 feet wide, the setback for the side yard would be 6.6 feet.
*      The house is 26 feet wide, subtract that from the 66 and you get 40 feet.
*      The side of the house closest to town looks to be 8 feet from the lot line.

*     The shed needs to be 10 feet from the side of the house, so subtract 10 feet

•     Subtract 6.6 feet from 22 and that leaves 15.4 feet for a shed.

Presentation by the Petitioner: Mr. Christoff confirmed that the Board members
had received the drawings of the property. All said that they have them. He stated
that the structure in question is so close to the road that it is a hazard which makes
it almost impossible to safely back out into the road without a spotter. They felt
that it would be safer to remove the shed and put up a different one in a safer
location which would require the variance in question. This new structure will be
narrower and smaller. The question is that the law requires it to be 30 feet from
the road right of way, but they are proposing that it be 12 feet from the right of
way. If they did as ZA Carey suggested to put the shed in the side yard to comply,
it would take away their safe and enjoyable side yard for their children and guests
to use. They feel that this side yard should be maintained as a recreational green
space and not used for a shed.

His next point is that there is a new house just down the road which has been built
only 10 feet from the road right away. This is a precedent.

He finished by stating that he appreciates the support that they have received from
neighbors.

An Explanation of this process is given to the applicant that after the ZBA makes
and accepts a motion to deny or approve the Variance Request the applicant may
wait to receive a final decision paper for court purposes. If the applicant does not
want to wait for the final decision paper, the decision will not be final until after
the next ZBA meeting. In the case of a hearing held via Zoom, the Certified
Decision Form will be emailed to the applicant in order to expedite the process.

Mr. Christoff addressed Ken & Marla Beougher concerning a practical difficulty
which exists due to safety issues. He is disappointed that Jerry and Ken don’t see a
practical difficulty. He brought up the house recently built as close to the road as
they are asking for.

McCaslin stated that you have to meet all the standards. ZA Carey stated that the
house in question was built in the exact footprint of the past structure. This does
not apply in this case as the ZA cannot grant something that is in the road right of
way.

Motions: McCaslin made a motion to deny the Christoffs’ request for variance at
1950 Forest Ave. for the construction of an accessory building. The request does
not comply with the Lincoln Township Ordinance #44, Section 20.9. This
proposed structure does not meet the guidelines for “Practical Difficulty”.
Swanson seconded.

Roll Call Vote: McCaslin: Yes; Swanson: Yes; Bridges: Yes; Beougher: Yes.
The motion passed by a vote of 4 to 0. The variance is denied.
