General Comments by Chair: Chair Godau explained the structure and functions
of the township government. He went on to explain what a variance involves and
how the zoning administrator deals with the steps of zoning permits, building
permits, and then the process in which a permit is denied. He also explained the
roles of the zoning administrator and the building inspector. The decision of the
zoning board of appeals shall be final.

A party aggrieved by the decision may appeal to the circuit court of Clare County
within whichever of the following deadlines comes first:
   a. Thirty days after the zoning board of appeals issues its decision in writing
      signed by the chairperson, if there is a chairperson, or signed by the
      members of the zoning board of appeals, if there is no chairperson.
   b. Twenty-one days after the zoning board of appeals approves the minutes of
      its decision.

Purpose: Chair Godau read the following information: This variance request of
Jeffery and Sandra Drew for their property at 1995 Bungo Trail, Lincoln
Township, Harrison, Michigan 48625, of Clare County, consisting of Lot 19,
Stiner Subdivision, Tax ID #010-520-019-00 and Parcel A, part of Lot 1, Block 1,
Oakdale Resort on Lake George, #010-321-001-03 is requesting approval for
construction of a deck, covered or uncovered, which as requested would directly
violate the Lincoln Township Zoning Ordinance No. 44.

At this time Chair Godau asked for a vote on the motion to approve the May 10,
2017, minutes. Motion carried 5 to 0.

Public notice: Notice of this Public Hearing was posted in the Clare County
Cleaver (Legal Section) on February 22, 2018. Notifications were sent to all
property owners within 300 feet of the property for the purpose of public comment
being made to the ZBA. The public hearing information was also posted at the
township hall and on the township’s website in the calendar of events.

Brief Explanation of the Rules: Chair Godau explained that variance requests are
submitted in person by the persons making the request. The Lincoln Township
Board of Appeals must follow the variance standards created by Michigan Statute.
These provide that the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in
order to grant a variance. These statutes ARE LAW and provide that: 1. There is a
Practical Difficulty; 2. The circumstances are Unique to the Property; 3. There is
Substantial Justice; 4. The issue was Not Self-Created; 5. Minimum Variance
Necessary; 6. That the strict compliance with the site development in questions
Zoning Board of Appeals Minutes            3                                  3/14/2018


would unreasonably prevent the owner from using the property; 7. That the
variance requested is the minimum amount necessary to overcome the inequality
inherent in the particular property. Throughout his preceding comments and
several times in the following discussions, Chair Godau reiterated that the purpose
of the ZBA was not to design, engineer, nor develop alternatives to the proposed
building project.

Statement from Zoning Administrator: ZA Jean Abbott stated Ordinance 44,
Article 20, Section 20.9 subsection H and Section 20.12 subsection B. She then
read the pertinent text which she used as the reason for the denial of this variance.

Petitioner’s Statement: Mr. Drew stated that they have owned the property for
several years along with the property with their residence. The two lots are
connected by taxes if not physically. Their intention is to better the property with
steps to the water. Mrs. Drew was injured in trying to get to the water last year so
they thought it would be wise to put in stairs and level areas. Then to make better
use of the lot, they desired to put in a small covered deck in order to have furniture
on it. They felt that this plan would be minimally invasive to the water and the
neighbors’ properties. The person who has the lot next to this one is fine with the
Drews’ plans as is another neighbor, Jody Woods. She was only concerned about
there being a roof that might impede her vision of the lake.

He feels that this property is virtually contiguous with the rest of his property due
to the shape of the lots and the roadway and driveway on his residence.

They are only attempting to do something with the hill to make it safe to use and
create a place to sit near the water. He didn’t expect this plan to be disapproved
because they have a deep enough lot and the plan will not impede anyone’s sight.

Don Hamilton of Lapham Associates, representing the Drews, made a statement
about the intent of zoning and then went on to say that he was confused by the
current ordinance concerning the 50 foot set back as he feels that many properties
on the lake are in noncompliance. He went on to say that this plan for the Drew
property will not harm anyone.

Chair Godau addressed the contiguous aspect of these two properties. He stated

McCaslin addressed the intent of the 50 foot setback and how it developed over the
years of discussions by the Planning Commission. The intent is to protect the
lakes. There are other things which can be done if they follow other rules like
height above the ground, etc. The biggest concerns are the environmental impact
on the lake. The properties that look like they are in nonconformity have been that
way for years; they are grandfathered in.

Chair Godau went on to reiterate the fact that other properties are closer to the lake
than currently allowed, but are grandfathered as they were created before the
zoning ordinance.

Hamilton stated that Lincoln’s ordinance has made it so older properties cannot be
rebuilt without coming before the ZBA for a variance. He said that the board is
denying to the Drews what everyone else around the lake has.

Bridges stated he has worked with zoning for many years and in different places.
He said that Michigan Zoning Act spells out the same laws for everyone. We just
updated the new ordinance to remove many old problems. We are obligated by
Michigan law to apply the ordinance to situations and make our decisions.

Engelhardt stated that she lives in a nonconforming home and says that she can
empathize with them. She walked down to the lake on their property and it is a

planned would have a roof and this will impede her view. She went on to say that
their properties are NOT contiguous. She agrees that there are safety issues with
the lot.

J. Drew responded to the environmental issue in that they need to walk down the
hill, creating a path thus causing erosion. The intent with the deck is to have a flat
space for safety and create a human made walkway to prevent erosion. They do
not leave things around. He stated that he is a strong advocate of the environment

S. Drew talked briefly about their attempt to use the hill in the most natural way

J. Drew said that he is basically asking to be able to build landings onto the stairs
