PUBLIC HEARINGS/SPECIAL PRESENTATIONS

. Middle Michigan Development Corporation (MMDC)

Kati Mora, President and CEO of MMDC presented the Board of
Commissioners with the 2024 MMDC Annual Report. President Mora also
provided an update with the strategic plan for 2025 — 2027. Their continued
mission is to support the economy across the Clare County community.
Allison Heinrich, Director of Business Development and Attraction
highlighted some of the key projects from 2025.

. Equalization - Pete Preston —- Apportionment Report

Pete Preston, Equalization Director, provided a brief explanation of the
Apportionment Report currently under consideration by the Board of
Commissioners, which must be approved before submission to the State Tax
Commission.

It was moved by Commissioner Hoefling, seconded by Commissioner
Housler to accept the Apportionment Report as presented and authorize the
Equalization Director to spread the tax roll, roll call vote revealed:

Yeas: (7) being Commissioners Gross, Haskell, Ambrozaitis, Kleinhardt,
Housler, Hoefling and Gilmore

Nays: (0)

Absent: (2) being Commissioners Fancon and Majewski

Motion carried.

CLERK/REGISTER OF DEEDS REPORT

Clerk and Register of Deeds Lori Mott reported that everything is in place for the
upcoming November 4, 2025 Special Election. Clare County residents who live in the
Harrison and Gladwin Community School Districts will have bond proposals on the
ballot. Preparation and training is underway for the 2026 elections with special focus
on emergency preparedness and security.

Clerk Mott informed the Commissioners that Dominion sold their voting equipment
business to Liberty Vote so Clare County’s equipment (which has not been replaced) is
now Liberty Vote equipment. Lastly, the Michigan Association of County Clerks
recently had a special meeting and unanimously opposed the Ranked Choice Voting
proposal largely in part because of its direct conflict with The State Constitution.

TREASURER’S REPORT

Treasurer Jenny Beemer-Fritzinger provided all the statutory reports to the
Commissioners. She reported that they are wrapping up the year end reports. 3,000
delinquent tax notices where recently sent out, which is an average number. Her
office is currently researching new dog license software as they are interested in
switching. They are also looking into switching the dog license expiration date to end
in the month and year of the rabies vaccination expiration date. The 2026 dog licenses
are currently being sold in her office.

CHAIRMAN’S REPORT

Chairman George Gilmore has attended his meetings. He stated that the
Administrator did a wonderful job settling the contract and preparing the budget for
next year.

VICE CHAIRMAN’S REPORT
Vice Chairman Rick Fancon was absent.
COMMISSIONER’S REPORT

Commissioner Hoefling reported that Franklin Township plans to switch to a new web
service provider due to upgrades. The Mid Michigan Community Action Agency
continues to monitor the government shutdowns since they rely on State and Federal
funding. He also reported that Hamilton Township continues to work on their park.

Commissioner Ambrozaitis reported that the Fall Fly-In was a great event. Eleven
aircraft participated including a Bell 505 helicopter from Flint. Airplanes flew in from
Alma, Pinconning, Cadillac, Flint and Owosso. He also thanked the twelve volunteers
and local businesses that supported the Fly-In event.

Lastly, Commissioner Ambrozaitis reported that he contacted Senator Hauck,
Representative Kunse and the Road Commission expressing safety concern for three
areas on Business Route 127 in Harrison.

Commissioner Housler thanked Commissioner Ambrozaitis for being transparent with
the Airport and for doing a good job. She also commented that the Clare County
Transit put in three Electric Vehicle (EV) charging stations. Redding Township now
has a full board and Freeman Township plans to hire a new supervisor this month.

Commissioner Kleinhardt announced that the first solar cells in Clare County will be
getting started in Arthur Township.

Commissioner Haskell reported that he recently attended a career quest at Saginaw
Valley hosted by Michigan Works. The Clare County high school sophomores in
attendance learned of alternatives to not getting a bachelor’s degree.

Commissioner Gross gave a brief report on Surrey Township and he shared that he
will be attending his Substance Abuse Disorder Policy Oversight Board meeting.

Commissioner Majewski was absent.
