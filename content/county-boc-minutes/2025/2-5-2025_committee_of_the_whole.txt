JUSTICE

1. Honorable Judge Farrell and Lisa Ashley - Recovery Court Discussion
Judge Farrell and Recovery Court Director Lisa Ashley presented an update
for the Clare and Gladwin County’s Recovery and Veterans Treatment Court
Programs. The programs are set to provide assistance to veterans and non-
veterans that appear before the courts with substance abuse related cases.
They encouraged the Board of Commissioners to attend a session held every
other Thursday so they can witness this great program.

3. Wage Discussion and Committee Recommendation
The Wage and Personnel Committee met to discuss the wages for the
Prosecuting Attorney. Clare County is substantially lower than average
compared to similar counties. The committee recommends three-year step
increases his first year increase would be to $105,000.00, second year
increase to $110,000.00 and third year increase to $115,000.00.
Commissioner Gilmore and Administrator Phelps confirmed that these wage
increases would not increase the Prosecutor’s budget. The Prosecuting
Attorney will take the increase from other areas of his budget.

It was moved by Commissioner Majewski, seconded by Commissioner Fancon
to accept the recommendations of the Wage Committee for the Prosecutor’s
office, roil call vote revealed:

Yeas: (8) being Commissioners Majewski, Fancon, Housler, Kleinhardt, Gross,
Gilmore, Hoefling and Haskell

Nays: (1) being Commissioner Ambrozaitis

Absent: (0)

Motion carried

4. Election Consolidation & Ballot Programming Discussion
Administrator Phelps outlined the election costs that are the responsibility
of the County and the local units. Ballot programming is a responsibility of
the local units. Historically the County Clerk’s office has programmed the
ballot and the County Commissioners have not charged the local units for
that service. Administrator Phelps confirmed that with the massive changes
in election laws that the previous duties associated with elections have
become more labor intensive.

Administrator Phelps reminded the Commissioners that in 2023 the Board
approved the hiring of an election coordinator because of the additional
election duties including the County Clerk’s office running early voting for
the local units. The Board of Commissioners then approved to pay for the
election consolidation for early voting throughout 2024 to ease the burden
on the local units.

Administrator Phelps provided the Commissioners with information
regarding all the costs the County paid and was later reimbursed by the
State, for early voting in 2024. She then combined those costs with a
proposed programming stipend to the County Clerk in the amount of
$5,004.00 per election and proposed a charge to the townships and cities as
follows:

$480.00 per election in which there will be early voting and programming of
ballots.

$278.00 per election in which there is no early voting and only programming
of ballots.

Administrator Phelps confirmed that she has reached out to all 18 local
clerks and heard back from 12 of them. All 12 local clerks want the County
Clerk to continue to program the ballots and they thought there was a good
chance their local boards would approve the proposed amounts.

Commissioner Majewski expressed concern regarding paying the County
Clerk a stipend to program the ballot at the county building while using
county equipment and performing a task in which the county paid for the
training. Administrator Phelps confirmed that the County Clerk is an
employee of the County and would be paid just like any other employee that
has to be sent to training to perform a job.

5. Maintenance Department
Administrator Phelps made the Board of Commissioners aware of a future
request for approval for a credit card for the Maintenance Department in the
amount of $3,000.00 and replacement of some courthouse windows.

6. Hiring Freeze
Commissioner Gilmore brought forward the recommendation of the Wage
and Personnei Committee for a county-wide hiring freeze because of the
uncertainty of federal and state budgeting and confirmed all employee
replacements will need to be approved by the Board of Commissioners.

It was moved by Commissioner Kleinhardt, seconded by Commissioner
Fancon to approve a hiring freeze until further notice, motion carried.

7. Check Signing
Commissioner Gilmore noted that the Wage and Personnel Committee
proposed that because the check signing is not a statutory duty of the
Clerk’s office the Board of Commissioners should turn the check signing
over to the Administrator and have the Clerk verify the checks. Clerk Lori
Mott opposed that recommendation citing checks and balances noting that
the Administrator handles purchasing, approving all department purchases
and as the Department Head of Accounts Payable, will now be controlling
the payment of all those invoices. Clerk Mott confirmed they would be
totally removing the checks and balances.

It was moved by Commissioner Fancon, seconded by Commissioner
Majewski to take check signing out of the Clerk’s office, roll call vote
revealed:

Yeas: (6) being Commissioners Majewski, Fancon, Housler, Kleinhardt,
Gilmore, and Haskell

Nays: (3) being Commissioners Ambrozaitis, Hoefling and Gross

Absent: (0)

Motion carried
