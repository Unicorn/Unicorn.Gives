PUBLIC HEARINGS /SPECIAL PRESENTATIONS

L. Office of Aeronautics, Michigan Department of Transportation
Hilary Hoose presented the Board of Commissioners with an Economic
Benefit Assessment for the Clare County Airport. The Clare County Airport
has a direct local output of over $967,000.00 and a statewide output impact
of over $1 million. Additionally, a total of over $2.2 million of state economic
impact is created by the airport. Hilary Hoose stated that the airport proves
to be a worthwhile economic generator.

A few businesses have expressed interest in coming to the Clare County
Airport including a business that builds race engines and a military
helicopter salvage business. She concluded her presentation by saying that
our community is fortunate to have an airport and losing the airport would
more than likely result in another airport not being able to be licensed.

3. Clare/Gladwin Community Corrections Grant
Charlie Pistro, Director of the Department of Corrections, Honerable Judge
Ambrozaitis — 55t Circuit Court and Honorable Judge Hovey — 55th Circuit
Court explained the need for the Board’s support for the Clare/Gladwin
Community Corrections program that they would like to establish. The
program has to provide appropriate sanctions and services with the goal of
reducing occurrence of repeat criminal offenses that result in incarceration.

Judge Ambrozaitis shared that they are focusing on a smali part of the
Community Corrections program — a pre-trial services program. This will not
compete with the Recovery Court, it will compliment it. The State funds the
entire program so no money comes out of the County budget. The Gladwin
County Board of Commissioners has already approved for them to proceed
with the application process. Judge Hovey added that this grant has been
available since 1988 and this is the first time Clare/Gladwin Counties have
applied.

2. Recovery Court/OPIOID
Chief Judge Josh Farrell explained to the Board of Commissioners the need
to expand the Recovery Court to include a Recovery Services Coordinator
position. He shared that they recently discovered a need to fill some gaps in
the community. The goal is to provide the assistance needed to the
individuals that are not in the criminal justice system. This position will
serve both Clare and Gladwin Counties.

It was moved by Commissioner Kleinhardt, seconded by Commissioner
Majewski to approve the expansion of the Recovery Court Program to better
address the increasing needs of individuals with Opioid Use Disorder and
co-occurring Substance Use Disorders. This expansion will include the
development of pre-trial services that connect individuals to evidence-based
treatment, supportive services and diversion or deflection strategies prior to
arrest or arraignment. Funding for these efforts will be allocated from the
Opioid Settlement Funds, roll call vote revealed:

Yeas: (9) being Commissioners Ambrozaitis, Fancon, Gilmore, Housler,
Haskell, Majewski, Kleinhardt, Hoefling and Gross

Nays: (0)

Absent: (0)

Motion carried.

TREASURER’S REPORT

Treasurer Jenny Beemer-Fritzinger provided all the statutory reports to the
Commissioners — the current cash on hand, investments, and fund balances as of May
20, 2025, as well as the cash summary by funds for the month of April. She included
a revenue trend report from 2009 — Projected FY 2025 and clarified that all figures on
this report are actual except in the last column, which is the projected budget as of
May 20, 2025. She also included a fund balance trend graph and explained that the
2020 COVID shut down and ARPA funding in 2021 helped reset the county

finances. They have reached and exceeded the goal of 35% for the fund balance as a
percent of expenses due to the COVID shut down, ARPA funding and the efficiencies
the new Administrator has put into place.

Treasurer Beemer-Fritzinger mentioned that the 2024 tax settlement checks were sent
out at the end of April and all millage entities (Transit, 911, Senior Services, schools,
libraries, local units, county, etc.) should be able to operate for the rest of the year.
She is working with the local unit treasurers to begin collecting the 2025 taxes. Her
office creates databases to match the local unit to prepare for the tax season.

Treasurer Beemer-Fritzinger announced a save the date — the first public tax reverted
land auction will be Friday, August 22, 2025. The auction will be online only and as of
now, 51 properties will be going to sale. Lastly, her office is working with the clerk’s
office to move toward e-tax certifications,
