b. It was moved by Commissioner Housler, seconded by Commissioner
Hoefling to approve a stipend of $9,000.00 per election for the Clare
County Clerk and a stipend of $5,000.00 per election for the Chief
Deputy Clerk/ Register.

Prior to moving directly to a vote, Clerk Mott asked to speak. With
permission form Chairman Haskell, Clerk Mott wanted everyone to fully
understand that if they voted no on the stipend, she would be handing
the programming off to the vendor ElectionSource. Chairman Haskell
asked Clerk Mott to hold off on this request until the Commissioners
could do a wage study and look into the issue deeper. Clerk Mott
reminded the Commissioners that she presented this request to them
four weeks prior, followed up two weeks later to see if anyone had
questions, sent an email another week later to see if anyone wanted to
meet or talk, providing the Commissioners with her cell phone and office
phone numbers. Clerk Mott also confirmed that she had provided a
quote from the vendor for programming, details on each of the 3 elections
for 2024 as a cost comparison and an attorney’s opinion from the County
Attorney stating the stipend was allowed.

Commissioner Gilmore questioned if the County Clerk’s Office has
always programmed the election. Clerk Mott indicated that the County
Clerk’s Office has always programmed the election, but with the
increased workload surrounding elections and increased non-statutory
duties, the County Clerk would like to be compensated for the additional
workload and that could be achieved by a stipend for programming.

Commissioner Fancon questioned why the County is handling early
voting for the townships/ cities if the State is reimbursing for early voting.
Clerk Mott explained that it is more than a financial issue, it also
includes the hardship of finding election inspectors for 9-days of early
voting and the extensive additional time involved in executing early
voting.
