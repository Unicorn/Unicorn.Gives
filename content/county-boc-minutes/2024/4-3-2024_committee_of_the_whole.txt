JUSTICE

1. Victims’ Rights Advocate Kim Keeley
Victims’ Rights Advocate Keeley informed the Board of Commissioners
that April is National Child Abuse Prevention month, Sexual Assault
Awareness and Prevention month and National Crime Victims week is at
the end of the month and handed out a calendar of events for Clare
County in April. She gave a presentation regarding the fundraiser they
hold for Crime Victims and explained that the funds are used in Clare
County for items such as cell phones without a plan, gas cards, clothing,
food, consumers bills and other items that are approved by the State of
Michigan to meet the direct needs of crime victims.

2. Animal Control — Rudi Hicks
Administrator Phelps asked Ms. Hicks to come to the meeting to answer
questions regarding tickets. Ms. Hicks confirmed that Assistant Prosecuting
Attorney previously instructed her to start writing tickets based on State
Law and then Administrator Phelps instructed her to go back to writing
tickets based on County Ordinance. Tickets are currently being written
based on County Ordinance.

County Administrator Phelps asked the Board of Commissioners to review
the 1989 ordinance and see if anything needs to be updated.

FINANCE AND ADMINISTRATION

1. 24-25 Revenue Preview
Administrator Phelps confirmed the County Budget is in good shape, but
won't be if the Commissioners don’t remain pro-active. Decreases in
revenue are because of court costs, marijuana crimes have disappeared and
criminal reform has crippled the courts in the ability to collect fines and
costs. Those two items amount to around $200,000.00. Register of Deeds
recording fees and real estate transfer tax will decrease about $125,000.00
because the sale price of property has decreased, which leads to less
transfer tax.

FINANCE AND ADMINISTRATION - continued

24-25 Revenue Preview - continued

Jail revenue is also decreasing in the amount of approximately $700,000.00
next year because of the decrease in federal inmates due to criminal reform.
The final decrease in revenue that is known right now is about $40,000.00
in interest earned from ARPA Funds that are now spent. The total revenue
decrease looks like about $2.5 million.

Administrator Phelps confirmed that she and the Sheriff are talking with
Osceola County and possible combining jails and Clare County would take
on Osceola County inmates. Osceola County would then pay Clare County
to house their inmates ($35 per day with an average of 55-60 inmates) and
Osceola County will pay for those inmates medical costs. She also looked at
combining with another county and housing Clare County inmates in a
different county. That did not save as much money as Clare County would
make by taking on Osceola County inmates.

24-25 Health Insurance Costs Preview

Each employee currently pays 2% of their insurance. Clare County
currently pays $2.5 million for the health insurance plan. Fiscal Year 24-25
Clare County will pay $2.7 million for health insurance.

Administrator Phelps gave scenarios for how costs would change for the
employee and the county:

a. If Clare County opts into PA152 hard cap the County cost
would be $2.1 million and the employee cost would be $966.00
per month for a family plan.

b. If the employees contributed 5% ($108.00 per month for a
family plan) instead of 2% it would cost the County $2.6
million.

c. Ifthe employees contributed 10% ($216.00 for a family pian)
instead of 2% it would cost the County $2.4 million.

Administrator Phelps discussed other decreases and increases to the
revenue. She believes Clare County will see a Headlee Rollback this year,
which will decrease tax revenue. The State has promised an increase in
revenue sharing in the amount of $400,000.00, if they actually follow
through. There is a ballot initiative called Axe the Tax, which would
eliminate property taxes all together. Union negotiations are beginning with
wage openers for 3 unions and open contracts for 6 unions.

Software Purchase — Elections

County Clerk Mott proposed the purchase of Election Night Reporting
software. The current reporting requires you to click on every precinct and
add results together to get the final total. The new software would add the
results together and show a graph and totals all in one spot for quick and
easy results. Clerk Mott confirmed that at the present time there is enough
money in the election budget for the purchase.

OPEN DISCUSSION

Administrator Phelps reported to the Board of Commissioners that she had some
complaints regarding having MMR run the Emergency Services Brainstorming
Meeting. She confirmed that the Clare County Board of Commissioners has no say on
what ambulance service comes into the county, the Medical Control Board makes that
decision. Administrator Phelps thought that if they all worked collectively as a group
and included the current EMS provider, MMR in the discussion, that everyone would
have knowledge of the issues and could help to fix the problem. The Clare County
Board of Commissioners and Administrator Phelps and only trying to facilitate a
meeting to try and fix the problems that exist with the providers in place.
