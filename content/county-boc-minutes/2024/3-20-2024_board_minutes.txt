PUBLIC HEARINGS /SPECIAL PRESENTATIONS

Marlana Terrian, E/911 Director

Director Terrian was present to give the Board of Commissioners an
overview of Emergency 9-1-1 and Dispatch. She went over the governance &
history, revenue streams, staffing (8 employees), duties and responsibilities,
challenges and upcoming projects.

Karl Hauser, Veterans Director

Director Hauser presented the annual report for Veterans Services, which
includes the different types of claims they help veterans with, an overview of
the financial impact to Clare County and the different types of assistance
they provide veterans. Director Hauser went into detail regarding the
services provided to veterans from the Soldier’s Relief Fund, Michigan
Veterans Trust Fund and burial allowance. Director Hauser confirmed that
Clare County also has a Veteran’s Treatment Court and explained the
different community outreach programs they participate in and the
Veteran’s Crisis Line.

Allisha Gary, assistant to the Veteran’s Director gave a report regarding the
veteran’s transportation network in which they transported 275 veterans in
2023 and the donations for basket items that are handed out to local
veterans.

New Dawn Shelter

Doug Lewis, Co-Director of New Dawn Shelter and Lanette Morgan were
present to explain the New Dawn Shelter that is available to Clare County
residents. Director Lewis explained the homeless shelter’s mission, case
management and funding, which is faith based and does not qualify for
state funding (they are funded by donations only). New Dawn Shelter has
limited the scope of services to Gladwin, Clare and Arenac Counties and are
based out of Gladwin County. The New Dawn Shelter performs a pretty
extensive criminal background check and require the participants to
participate in case management and treatment programs if necessary. The
core philosophy is to offer a hand up and not a hand out. New Dawn Shelter
works with Community Mental Health, Mid-Michigan Community Action
Agency, Department of Health and Human Services and the court system.
The shelter is volunteer driven and they are very careful to protect guests
and staff. Director Lewis confirmed that almost half of people in their
shelter come from Clare County.

CLERK/REGISTER OF DEEDS REPORT

Clerk and Register of Deeds Lori Mott gave an update regarding early voting. Clerk
Mott reported that voters utilized the early voting location for all 18 jurisdictions
located in Clare County. Clerk Mott outlined some of the ways she saved Clare
County $75,265.00 during early voting (early voting equipment support $18,000.00,
programming the ballot $23,265.00 and early voting notification postcards
$34,000.00).

TREASURER’S REPORT

Treasurer Jenny Beemer-Fritzinger provided all the statutory reports to the
Commissioners and the following updates

Treasurer Beemer-Fritzinger and her staff are working on delinquent tax settlement of
2023 taxes. They reconciled a little over $53 million in expected revenues of which
approximately $5.4 million or 10% are delinquent. They currently have three years of
active delinquent taxes in her office. The 2023’s mentioned above. The 2022
delinquent taxes just went into forfeiture meaning they are over one year

delinquent. There were approximately 1100 properties that went into forfeiture that
will be sent to the Register of Deeds office to be recorded.
