Explanation of Practical Difficulty: Chair McCaslin read the following brief
explanation of the “practical difficulty” requirements needed for a person to
request a zoning variance:

The Lincoln Township Board of Appeals must follow the variance standards
created by Michigan Statute and Ordinance 44 of Lincoln Twp. In accordance
with these standards the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in
order to grant a variance. Practical Difficulties as identified in Section8.7 of the
Lincoln Township Zoning Ordinances ARE THE LAW. They are as follows:
Zoning Board of Appeals Minutes            3                                8/10/2022


                   A. Practical Difficulty: That there are practical difficulties that
                      prevent carrying out the strict letter of this Ordinance due to
                      unique circumstances specific to the property such as its
                      narrowness, shallowness, shape, or topography, and which do
                      not generally apply to other property or uses in the same
                      district and shall not be recurrent in nature. That a genuine
                      hardship exists due to the circumstances are Unique to the
                      Property
                   B. Circumstances: That the practical difficulty or special
                      condition or circumstance is not a result of the actions of the
                      applicant. That the variance will relate only to property
                      described in the variance application. That granting the
                      variance will not grant an applicant any special privilege.
                   C. Ordinance Purpose Adherence: Does this request relate to
                      properties other than described in the Variance request?
                   D. Variance Harmony: That the variance will be in harmony with
                      the purpose of this Ordinance and the intent of the District,
                      including the protection of public health, safety and welfare in
                      general and vehicular and pedestrian circulation specifically.
                   E. Effects: That the variance will not cause a substantial adverse
                      effect upon surrounding property including property values
                      and the development, use and enjoyment of property in the
                      neighborhood or District.
                   F. Property Use: That strict compliance with the site
                      development requirement in question would unreasonably
                      prevent the owner from using the property for a permitted
                      purpose or would render conformity unnecessarily
                      burdensome.
                   G. Minimum Variance: That the variance requested is the
                      minimum amount necessary to overcome the inequality
                      inherent in the particular property or mitigate the practical
                      difficulty.

Purpose: The purpose of this hearing is to determine if indicators exist to grant a
variance as the zoning permit filed by the residents on August 19, 2020,
was denied. Application for a variance was received on May 19 , 2022, applicant
Warren Schobert for property located at 101 Maple Street , Tax ID#010-400-001-
00. The following is the reason for the variance, to allow a wood privacy fence in
the backyard, which is out of compliance with the Lincoln Township Zoning
Ordinance due to it being considered a corner lot.

Correspondence & Communications to ZBA: None

Presentation by Lincoln Township Zoning Administrator:
ZA Hassberger explained that he worked from files that had been prepared by
former ZA Carey.

1.      The fence in question was erected without a Zoning Permit. At the time of
installation, the fence was non-conforming in size and placement.
2.     It appears, from the file documents, that the applicant was less than
cooperative in correcting the violations that he created.
3.     Fence, as it stands, may be detrimental to the neighbors to the north
4.     Former ZA Carey, in 2019, interpreted the ordinance using the Section 21
definition of Corner Lot Frontage.
5.     Ordinance 44 as amended in 2021 clarifies Triple Lot Frontages and does
nothing to bring this fence into compliance.

There were no questions for ZA Hassberger

Presentation by the Petitioner: Nicole Speer spoke for the applicant, Warren
Schobert.

She explained that ZA Carey noted that their circumstance is very unusual. They
put the fence up because they wanted to keep their 5 year old son safe well as have
privacy. They did move the fence back.

ZBA Members Comments & Questions: No questions from the board.

Motion: McCaslin made the motion to deny the applicant, Warren Schobert’s
request at 101 Maple Street, Lake, Michigan 48632 for a privacy fence. The
request does not comply with the Lincoln Township Ordinance 44, Section 20.11:
Fences and Walls, and is considered a corner lot with 3 fronts, Section 20.15: Clear
Vision Zones. The board finds that the proposed structure does not meet the
guidelines for practical difficulty. Bridges seconded

Roll Call Vote: Bridges: Yes; Keller: Yes; Lease: Yes; McCaslin: Yes; Swanson:
