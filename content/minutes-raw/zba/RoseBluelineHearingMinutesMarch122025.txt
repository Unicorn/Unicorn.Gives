Explanation of Practical Difficulty: Chair McCaslin read the following brief
explanation of the practical difficulty requirements needed for a person to request a
zoning variance:

The Lincoln Township Board of Appeals will follow the variance standards created
by Michigan Statute and Ordinance 44 of Lincoln Township. In accordance with
these standards the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in order
to grant a variance. Practical Difficulties as identified in Section 8.7 of the Lincoln
Township Zoning Ordinances ARE THE LAW. They are as follows:

                  A. Practical Difficulty: That there are practical difficulties that
                     prevent carrying out the strict letter of this Ordinance due to
                     unique circumstances specific to the property such as its
                     narrowness, shallowness, shape, or topography, and which do
                     not generally apply to other property or uses in the same

                     district and shall not be recurrent in nature. That a genuine
                     hardship exists due to the circumstances are Unique to the
                     Property.
                  B. Circumstances: That the practical difficulty or special
                     condition or circumstance is not a result of the actions of the
                     applicant. The variance will relate only to property described
                     in the variance application. That granting the variance will not
                     grant an applicant any special privilege.
                  C. Ordinance Purpose Adherence: Does this request relate to
                     properties other than described in the Variance request?
                  D. Variance Harmony: That the variance will be in harmony
                     with the purpose of this Ordinance and the intent of the
                     District, including the protection of public health, safety and
                     welfare in general and vehicular and pedestrian circulation
                     specifically.
                  E. Effects: That the variance will not cause a substantial adverse
                     effect upon surrounding property including property values
                     and the development, use and enjoyment of property in the
                     neighborhood or District.
                  F. Property Use: That strict compliance with the site
                     development requirement in question would unreasonably
                     prevent the owner from using the property for a permitted
                     purpose or would render conformity unnecessarily
                     burdensome.
                  G. Minimum Variance: That the variance requested is the
                     minimum amount necessary to overcome the inequality
                     inherent in the particular property or mitigate the practical
                     difficulty.

Presentation by Lincoln Township Zoning Administrator:
Zoning Administrator Hassberger presented his reasons for denying the Zoning
Permit. He read:

On January 20, 2025, a completed Application for Zoning Variance was received
from Mr. Hollingshead.

Based on my visit to the property, the drawings and scale provided by Blueline,
and information from Property Records, the following failures to meet or exceed
the Setback Requirements of Zoning Ordinance #44 exist.

       1. The main portion of the proposed deck would be set back 44 feet from the
front of the lot. A setback of 50 feet is required.
       2. The stairway portion of the proposed deck would be set back 29 feet from
the front of the lot. A setback of 50 feet is required.
       3. Based on the drawings submitted, the proposed deck and stairway would
be 33 feet wide. The lot is 40 feet wide. This would result in a 3 ½ foot setback if
centered. The side setback required is 5 feet.
       4. The ordinance requires a setback of 10 feet between any structures on a
lot. The plan indicated a setback of only 6 feet from the Shed/Boathouse.
       5. Additionally, Ordinance #44 limits the Building Coverage in the R2
Zoning District to 25% of the lot area. Based on the plans submitted and property
records, the proposed construction would exceed that percentage and would
increase Building Coverage to 28%.

In addition to the above, the drawing indicates a new gable roof on the
Shed/Boathouse which was not a part of the Zoning Application. This roof would
not be allowed as it would increase the area of non-conformity of the existing non-
conforming Shed/Boathouse.

The Zoning Administrator cannot support the Application for Zoning Variance.

Presentation by the Petitioner: Mr. Hollingshead reviewed the history of the
project concerning safety. His proposal is to give them safe access to the boat
house with a new outdoor living space which would make it easier to get from the
house to the boathouse and deck.

ZBA Members Comments & Questions:

Keasey asked what type of roof was planned for; answer a low roof. She also
