Explanation of Practical Difficulty: Chair McCaslin read the following brief
explanation of the “practical difficulty” requirements needed for a person to
request a zoning variance:

The Lincoln Township Board of Appeals will follow the variance standards created
by Michigan Statute and Ordinance 44 of Lincoln Township. In accordance with
these standards the ZBA must determine that a Practical Difficulty exists in order
to grant a variance. Practical Difficulties as identified in Section 8.7 of the Lincoln
Township Zoning Ordinances ARE THE LAW. They are as follows:

                  A. Practical Difficulty: That there are practical difficulties that
                     prevent carrying out the strict letter of this Ordinance due to
                     unique circumstances specific to the property such as its
Zoning Board of Appeals Minutes           3                               11/13/2024


                     narrowness, shallowness, shape, or topography, and which do
                     not generally apply to other property or uses in the same
                     district and shall not be recurrent in nature. That a genuine
                     hardship exists due to the circumstances are Unique to the
                     Property.
                  B. Circumstances: That the practical difficulty or special
                     condition or circumstance is not a result of the actions of the
                     applicant. The variance will relate only to property described
                     in the variance application. That granting the variance will not
                     grant an applicant any special privilege.
                  C. Ordinance Purpose Adherence: Does this request relate to
                     properties other than described in the Variance request?
                  D. Variance Harmony: That the variance will be in harmony with
                     the purpose of this Ordinance and the intent of the District,
                     including the protection of public health, safety and welfare in
                     general and vehicular and pedestrian circulation specifically.
                  E. Effects: That the variance will not cause a substantial adverse
                     effect upon surrounding property including property values
                     and the development, use and enjoyment of property in the
                     neighborhood or District.
                  F. Property Use: That strict compliance with the site
                     development requirement in question would unreasonably
                     prevent the owner from using the property for a permitted
                     purpose or would render conformity unnecessarily
                     burdensome.
                  G. Minimum Variance: That the variance requested is the
                     minimum amount necessary to overcome the inequality
                     inherent in the particular property or mitigate the practical
                     difficulty.

Denial of Zoning Application: The requested structure does not meet setback
requirements.

Purpose: The purpose of this hearing is to determine if indicators exist to grant a
variance as the zoning permit filed by the residents on October 7, 2024, was denied
due to the required setbacks not being met. Application for a variance was made
on September 4, 2024, by Royal Miller of 11205 Glennmark Trail, Montrose,
Michigan 48457, to allow construction of a pavilion out of compliance with
setback requirements.

Correspondence & Communications to ZBA: Chair McCaslin read an email
communication from a neighbor which was addressed to Clerk Majewski.

Presentation by Lincoln Township Zoning Administrator:
ZA Hassberger presented his background information with the Millers and their
inability to operate the retractable awning currently being used. ZA Hassberger
suggested a motorized awning.

Presentation by the Petitioner: Mr. Miller then explained the situation at his
property and that a past injury has made it impossible for him to operate his current
awning. He then described the proposed construction which would consist of two
permanent posts for supporting an awning. Mrs. Miller added that the reason that
the pavilion posts would have to go out beyond the existing cement pad is in order
to sink them below the frost line. The awning would then become a stationary
pavilion.

ZBA Members Comments & Questions:
Simons asked if the pavilion would be attached to the house. Miller answered that
it will be attached to the house.

McCaslin asked for clarification of the measurements of the deck and the distance
from the lakefront.

McCaslin asked about the height of the new structure which will cover a 16 by 18
foot section of the deck.

Kalat asked about how long the deck has been there; it has been there since before
the Millers purchased the property.
