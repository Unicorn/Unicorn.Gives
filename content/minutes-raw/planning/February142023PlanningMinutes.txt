Public Hearing: Chair Blaisdell opened the public hearing concerning the removal
of the R-2A zoning designation.

Chair’s Opening Remarks:

In 2016 the Township worked on updates to ordinance 44.

New language addressed camping on lots as any 30 days in a 90-day period.

Concern was raised by White Birch representatives that the new policy was too
restrictive and would destroy White Birch as they knew it.

After much debate and discussion, a solution to create a new residential zoning
district for White Birch sections called R2A.

R2A allowed camping on vacant lots in WB for a specific period May 1 st through
October 31st without permit. At the time this opportunity was not extended to the
entire township.

In 2017 the new ordinance and zone was adopted by the township.

In 2021 residents approached the township requesting temporary sheds throughout
the camping season. Lincoln Township reviewed the whole camping policy and
amended the zoning ordinance to allow the use of a total 240 square foot
temporary sheds during the camping season. They also extended the camping
season 1 extra month to December 1st and approved the entire township can camp
on vacant lots May 1st through December 1st with approved permit.

The Lincoln Township Planning Commission is now entertaining the possibility of
recommending removal of the R2A zoning district within the township as it no
longer serves the purpose it was intended as relates to community zoning with R2
& R2A which are now the same and there is no need for a separate zoning
designation within the township.

The planning commission has scheduled a public hearing tonight February 14 th, to
take public comments regarding the R2A zoning district.

Please raise your hand to be recognized to speak.

Please state your name for the record. We may ask you to spell it for the record.

Please address all comments to the Planning Commission Chair which is me.

In the spirit and interest of time, please be to the point with your comment or
concern.

If you have sent a letter to the township with your opinion, it is not necessary to
repeat it, however if you feel compelled to do so the commission will listen.

If any commissioner wants to address a topic or answer a question, please raise
your hand to be recognized by the chair.

Please recognize that there may be differing opinions presented tonight and I ask
that we all be professional and respectful of each other and allow those who want
to speak to do so without interruption.

Old Business:
1. Updating Master Plan progress: Chair Blaisdell has received communication with his
Wade Trim contact concerning progress on their part. They plan to attend the March 14

2. R-2A – R-2 Zoning Map Districts Amendment Discussion:
Chair Blaisdell read his statement in support of deferring the decision until the Master
Plan process begins and let that process make the necessary change if any. Tobin
agrees with this position.

McCaslin stated her position that R-2A was the correct solution at the time, but when
the amendment was approved to give the rest of the township the same camping
opportunities, she feels that there is no longer a need for the R-2A zone and those
references no longer have any meaning. She addressed the concern about what if the
commission changes everything in six months; the commission would not eliminate
camping on residential lots. She reiterated that there is nothing being taken away from
White Birch; it is just to remove confusion.

Tobin made it clear that he does not want to make any votes with only three members
present.

Chair Blaisdell then read the following statement of his opinion:

“I am not convinced that retaining the R2A would protect camping rights in the future.
If a catastrophic event happened to change the minds of the community that camping
needed to be adjusted should rest solely on concerns of the time, not based on figuring
out the intent of why it was left in place even though it serves no purpose and should
have been removed. We just experienced this with the R1 – R2 issue on Lake George in
which a section was zoned R1 even though none of the properties conformed to
R1. Much confusion, time and work went in to trying to find out if it was a mistake, was
there some reason why it was R1 even though properties did not conform. It frustrated
everyone because it was not correct, never was and caused confusion that did not have
to happen.

I am a firm believer in our process in which we govern. As example in 2016 – 2017
when the R2a was being discussed as a solution to White Birch camping, there were
several in the township that argued it was a bad idea. One member who is the lighting
                    Lincoln Township Is An Equal Opportunity Provider

rod currently for making the comment “I hate campers” which some took out of
context, at the time this was all being debated this commissioner was the chair
of the planning commission. I believe the position has not changed regarding camping
on vacant lots however the R2A zone was adopted despite the strong opposition. The
process proved it worked. We must believe in it.

In reading the letters and listening to the comments throughout this process there are
clearly two distinct sides to this issue. As I read letters for instance for those who feels
strongly to keep the R2A the overriding theme was “If it isn’t broke don’t fix it”. It
makes no sense to remove it. Very clear message and very direct. One could easily
argue that and form a clear decision. Then I read letters that states it no longer serves a
purpose and I can’t understand why you would keep it? It needs to be removed. Very
clear message and very direct. How does one decide when you are charged to represent
both opinions?

Recently I had a conversation with someone who I believe provided a positive “value
added” perspective that I had not heard with regards to the R2A designation. Even
though it is not currently designated for anything other than length of time to camp, it
was suggested that I think about how the R2a could potentially benefit the association /
township down the road. The gated association is recognized for provide amenities i.e.,
clubhouse, private campground, indoor pool, warming house for students waiting
for the school bus, private beaches and privately maintained roads for its members and
guests and not extended but accepted by most township residents. If the association
decided in the future to enhance those amenities or possibly develop new amenities
might be easier to accomplish approval through the recognized R2A zone in
comparison to try and secure a special use permit. I do not know enough about the
mechanics of the process, and I am not fully sure this is a correct line of thinking. In
theory this does make sense if it is correct. I am not in favor of giving one group of
residents’ favor over others in the township and would never support any effort to try to
do so, however this association is self-supporting other than police, fire, and rescue. It
should be easier to secure approval on such things to enhance the association.

As a commission we are charged to decide the direction to take. I am of the current
thinking that because the township has started the process of our much-needed update to
our master plan this is the place to work through all of this. Short of a motion until I
hear the thoughts of the rest of the commission, I personally would like to defer the
decision on next step until after the master plan has been completed to give us clearer
vision and a road map with guidance for the best interest of our community.”

Chair Blaisdell then made a motion to defer and table the next step in the Planning
Commission’s decision process of potential removal of the R2A Zoning designation
until after the Master Plan has been completed and adopted by Lincoln Township which
will most likely be sometime in the year of 2024.

Tobin seconded. Motion passed with 2 yeas and 1 nay.

Motion by McCaslin to adjourn the meeting; seconded by Tobin. Motion passed
