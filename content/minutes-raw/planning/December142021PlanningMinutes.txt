            PUBLIC HEARING - 14 December, 2021 - 7:00 p.m.                MINUTES

       The Public Hearing was called to order by Hassberger, at 7:03 p.m. Members present
included: Hassberger, Bridges, Szczepanski, McCaslin, and Tobin. Absent were Carey
(excused/Medical), Trim (excused/Family Issues), and Sherrod (excused). There were
sixteen (16) other persons, present to participate in the Hearing process. Dennis Zimmerman
was present, solely for the purpose of taking and recording minutes, in the absence of Sherrod.
Hassberger led those present in the Pledge of Allegiance.

        Hassberger explained the purpose of the Hearing was to receive, from the public, and
from interested persons, comments and suggestions regarding the proposed amendment(s) to
the current Lincoln Township Zoning Ordinance, specifically Section 20.8 “Recreational Vehicles
as Temporary Dwellings on Vacant Lots”, and Section 20.29 “Temporary Storage Structures”.

       Hassberger further explained the requested protocols for the Hearing as follows:
        Each person wanting to address the Planning Commission, would be allowed to
          speak, but must address all comments to the Chair. Cross conversations between
          attendees would not be allowed; and,
        Each person wanting to address the Planning Commission, would be allowed three
          (3) minutes for their resp. remarks; and,
        If time allowed a person may be allowed to speak a second time; and,
        Persons wishing to have exhibits entered into the Hearing would be allowed to do so.

Exhibits accepted for the Hearing, included:

       1.      Text, regarding the proposed Carey amendments to the Zoning Ordinance,
               Appr. 2 pages in length                       (labeled as exhibit PH-1)


       2.      Suggested alternate amendments, including seven (7) “Priorities” and covering
               appr. 2 ½ pages, from Tim Vajcner;               (labeled as exhibit PH-2)


       3.      A letter supporting the proposed Carey Zoning Amendments, along with a brief
               history of events and meetings leading to these proposals, slightly more than 1
               page in length, from Phil Blaisdell.                (labeled as exhibit PH-3)

Comments received from Members of the Public, included:

           Carey’s proposed amendments are an improvement, but could go farther;
           Current zoning is camper “Unfriendly”;
           Temporary canopies should be excluded from any regulation;
           Two temporary storage structures should be allowed, and that storage should be
            defined as for equipment, tools, toys, golf carts, UTV’s, motorcycles, bicycles, etc.;
           Temporarily placed Utility Trailers should be excluded from regulation;
           The size (ft2) should be increased from 120 ft2, to at least 200 ft2;
           A Minimum Size should be defined for storage structures, to be counted toward
            the maximum allowed on a lot;
           Permitting requirements should be eliminated;
           The number of current violations is disappointing, and W.B. Property Owners should
            be better “stewards”;
           In agreement with the proposed Vajcner suggestions;
           In agreement with the proposed Carey amendments;
           W.B. persons need to be made aware of violation parameters, and possible fines;
           How would these regulations be enforced in real time?
           Permits give the township, especially the Assessor more knowledge of individual lot
            improvements, specifically regarding Wells, Septic System and Electrical installation;
           Violations and Enforcement need more “Teeth”;
           Approve of 2-per-lot Camper limitations, and accompanying temporary structures;
           How many campers typically appear during the camping season?
           There are appr. 248 campers left on-site during the camping season;
           How many Property Owners are there in Total ?

Having appeared that every person wanting to speak, had that opportunity, Hassberger, then
closed the comment period and the Public Hearing at 7:31 p.m.

Old Business

       Hassberger opened discussion among the Planning Commission Members, regarding
the Proposed Amendments to Section 20.8 and Section 20.29 (i.e., the Carey proposed
amendments). P.C. Members offered insight and comments, including:

         The proposed amendments are “more than fair”;
         Agree with excluding canopy restrictions;
         W.B. Covenants & Restrictions are more strict that Township Zoning;
         W.B. was originally intended to be a gated community with upscale Housing Plans
          and unique amenities;
         W.B. was not intended to be a campground;
         Economically, revenues from W.B. are generated by Homeowners, not campers;
         Encouraging campers is not in the best interest of W.B. or Lincoln Township;
         Would like to see (and support) a reduction of “clutter” on Residential Lots;
         Why not store utility trailers in one of the two storage areas provided by W.B.
         The R2-A Zoning District could and should be extinguished, and the Township’s
          regulation of limiting a camper placement for 30 days could be revisited;
         Any amendment to allow more camping is not what was originally intended by the
          W.B. Platters;
         Permitting would result in better tracking and better control of camper placement;
         Permitting would allow for better compliance of Set-backs, Unlicensed vehicles;
         Should a permit ever be denied, such as with a violation of Set-backs, etc.?
         Amendments would allow W.B. to be a bit more camper-friendly, with regard to utility
          trailer, temporary storage structures, and canopies;
